Questions

How do you find bed bugs in Florida homes?

Inspectors start at the mattress and box spring, checking seams, tufts, and piping for live bugs, eggs, or dark fecal spots. They move to the headboard, bed frame, and baseboards, using flashlights and magnifying tools. Next they examine nearby furniture, outlets, and wall voids. In Florida homes they also check luggage storage areas and behind picture frames. Every room along the sleeping path gets checked, including sofas and recliners where people nap.

What areas in Florida are most affected by bed bugs?

High-traffic tourist zones bring the most reports. Miami-Dade, Orlando, and Tampa see frequent introductions through airports, cruise ports, and hotels. College towns like Gainesville and Tallahassee also record spikes during move-in weeks. Interstate corridors such as I-95 and I-4 carry travelers who unknowingly transport bugs between cities. Short-term rental properties near beaches show steady activity year-round.

How long does bed bug treatment take in West Palm Beach?

An initial inspection usually lasts one to two hours. Once confirmed, a standard treatment appointment takes three to four hours for an average apartment or condo. Heat applications can run six to eight hours depending on room volume. Follow-up visits are scheduled seven to ten days later and typically finish within two hours. Total timeline from first call to final clearance is about three weeks when two treatments are needed.

Can bed bugs return after treatment in Florida?

Yes, they can return if new bugs are brought in or if missed eggs hatch. Technicians schedule at least one follow-up visit to catch emerging nymphs. Residents should launder bedding on arrival, inspect luggage after travel, and avoid secondhand furniture. Sealing cracks and reducing clutter removes future hiding spots. Ongoing monitoring with interceptors under bed legs helps catch problems before they spread again.

Are heat treatments effective against bed bugs in Florida?

Heat kills every life stage when rooms reach 120 °F for at least 90 minutes. In Florida homes, technicians raise temperatures to 130–140 °F to offset cooler exterior walls. Mattresses, sofas, and wall voids all reach lethal levels without chemicals. The method leaves no residue and works in one visit for most single-family homes. Electronics and certain plastics must be removed beforehand to avoid damage.
