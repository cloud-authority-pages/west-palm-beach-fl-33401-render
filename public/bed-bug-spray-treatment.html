Questions

How does bed bug spray treatment work?

Technicians apply EPA-approved sprays directly to cracks, crevices, and harborages where bed bugs hide. The spray contacts both adult bugs and eggs, killing them on contact or through residual activity. By focusing on these hiding spots rather than open surfaces, the treatment reaches the insects where they actually live and breed, disrupting the entire population in a single visit.

Is bed bug spray treatment safe for my family?

When applied by trained professionals, the treatment meets strict safety guidelines for indoor use. Technicians use products labeled for residential settings and apply them only in targeted areas, away from food surfaces and out of reach of children and pets. Homeowners can safely return once the spray has dried, usually within a few hours.

How long does bed bug spray treatment take?

A typical West Palm Beach visit lasts one to two hours depending on the size of the infestation. The technician inspects all sleeping areas, furniture, and baseboards before applying the spray. Larger homes or heavy infestations may require additional time to ensure every harborage is treated thoroughly.

Will one bed bug spray treatment be enough?

Follow-up applications are often scheduled two weeks later to target newly hatched nymphs. A single spray kills adults and some eggs, but surviving eggs can hatch within 10–14 days. The second visit ensures those nymphs are eliminated before they mature and lay new eggs.

What should I do before Bed Bug Exterminator arrives?

Remove clutter, wash bedding in hot water, and clear access to baseboards and mattress edges. Decluttering reduces hiding spots and lets the technician reach all affected areas. Washing linens kills bugs and eggs already present. Clearing pathways around furniture speeds up the inspection and treatment process.
