Heat Treatment vs. Chemical Methods for Bed Bug Control

Choosing between heat and chemicals comes down to the specific conditions of each property rather than a one-size-fits-all preference. Heat treatment raises room temperatures above 120 degrees Fahrenheit, killing every life stage on contact without leaving residues. This method works particularly effectively in bedrooms and living areas where furniture can be moved away from walls. However, heat cannot penetrate wall voids or reach items that would be damaged by high temperatures. Chemical applications use products registered by the Florida Department of Agriculture & Consumer Services for bed bug control. These formulas are applied to cracks, baseboards, and mattress seams where bugs travel at night. The residual effect continues working between visits, which is essential because eggs can take up to two weeks to hatch. Most properties benefit from a combination of both approaches. Heat clears the visible population quickly while chemicals address hidden eggs and prevent re-infestation from adjacent units. Our technicians evaluate factors like construction type, furniture density, and resident health concerns before recommending the final plan. This tailored decision prevents the common mistake of using only one method and leaving part of the population untouched.
